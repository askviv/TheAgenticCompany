# Scenario: engineering-company admin/accounts assistant

Generate a realistic fictional company and a set of everyday problems for the
following job. Use the [shared sandbox authoring instructions](AUTHORING_AGENT.md)
to turn the scenario into a company pack. Keep the result grounded in ordinary
business work, with connected people, documents and events.

## The company

Create a small UK engineering supplier or contractor with several active customer
jobs. Explain what it sells or delivers, how jobs move through the business, who
is responsible for them and how the office works with customers, suppliers and
the people doing the work. Give the company a clear persona, separate from the
job below. Use fictional business information throughout.

## The job description

This scenario is inspired by the Admin/Accounts Assistant listing at
[BRACE Engineering](https://uk.indeed.com/viewjob?jk=85bc2b52b95ce5ab).
Use its responsibilities as inspiration, without claiming to reproduce the real
employer's records or internal systems.

The employee supports accurate financial administration and day-to-day office
operations:

- Enter information accurately and prepare documents and correspondence.
- Process the purchase ledger, match delivery notes and enter invoices.
- Follow up outstanding customer payments professionally by phone and email.
- Handle incoming calls, mail, filing and general administrative queries.
- Support colleagues and keep clear, organised records.

The role needs Word and Excel proficiency, accuracy, organisation, a confident
telephone manner and experience in a busy office or basic accounts role.
Knowledge of Sage is preferred, but training can be provided.

## What to generate

Create several related engineering jobs or customer orders, with realistic
customers, suppliers, deliveries, invoices, due dates and responsible colleagues.
Include everyday administration as well as problems to investigate: a delivery
note that does not match an invoice, duplicate or incorrect invoice information,
an overdue customer balance and an incoming query needing a colleague's input.

Show both ways people work: using company systems directly, and taking information
into a spreadsheet, checking it, then updating a record or sending a response.
Include source documents, emails, spreadsheets and an organised place for working
notes. Give the employee enough evidence to investigate, but leave genuine
uncertainties for them to resolve with colleagues.

The sandbox can represent job/project tasks, orders, deliveries, accounts, shared
documents, spreadsheets, email and chat. Use the existing tools that suit the
work. A written call note can provide context, but do not pretend a phone call
has happened when the simulation cannot make one.

Leave the work for the employee to do. Provide separate private reference answers
and expected evidence so someone else can judge the outcome. Deliver the finished
company pack and a short, plain-language explanation of the company, the work
included and anything the simulation cannot demonstrate. Do not run it or change
the live company.

## Possible construction adaptation

This type of office/accounts work could also take place at a small construction
or subcontracting company. If the operator asks for that variation, use sites or
jobs, material deliveries, subcontractor invoices, approved labour inputs and
payment queries as the business context. Do not assume the engineering employer
above is a construction company. Generate the engineering scenario by default;
construction is an alternative, not an extra pack to produce automatically.
