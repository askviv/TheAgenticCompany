These are two independent Python applications, not installable services with a shared adapter contract. CoffeeBench is packaged with Hatchling/pyproject; E-CommerceBench is a script-style repository with `run.py` and a requirements file. Pin each dependency set and verify the actual resolver/runtime before combining processes; no compatibility should be inferred from nominally common Python.

## What can be reused

### CoffeeBench: actor cadence, B2B interaction pattern, and ledger concepts

- **Concrete reuse candidate — scheduler data structure.** `coffeebench/event_loop.py` implements a min-heap of `(virtual_min, insertion_counter, Event)`, equal-time deterministic ordering, event buckets, `schedule_at`, `pop`, `peek_time`, and a virtual-minute clock. It is a useful small source-level candidate for event ordering/scheduler semantics, but is coupled to CoffeeBench event kinds and its environment handlers. A coordinator adapter still needs canonical event IDs/types, persistence, idempotency, and authoritative event ownership. [Source](https://github.com/SakanaAI/CoffeeBench/blob/9e2f3950b5812c8753de81ede9c68babc024b5c8/coffeebench/event_loop.py)
- **Concrete reuse candidate — bilateral messaging and offer lifecycle.** `coffeebench/marketplace.py` implements listing/offer/deal records, validates/accepts offers, and records sender/recipient messages; `BusinessApp` exposes actor-facing tools, including `send_message`. Messages are addressed to participants and `messages_visible_to` filters to sender or recipient. This is a useful interaction pattern for C1, but message identity/UUIDs, in-memory state, and its coffee `Deal`/invoice callback are not the target contract. It is not an email adapter. [Marketplace source](https://github.com/SakanaAI/CoffeeBench/blob/9e2f3950b5812c8753de81ede9c68babc024b5c8/coffeebench/marketplace.py) · [business app](https://github.com/SakanaAI/CoffeeBench/blob/9e2f3950b5812c8753de81ede9c68babc024b5c8/coffeebench/business_app.py)
- **Implemented selective reuse — independent supplier turns and company economy.** `coffeebench/agent.py` remains vendored for supplier turns. A separate company background worker constructs the complete pinned upstream environment, preserves native dispatch, native actor models, deal and invoice lifecycle, and private checkpoint state. It projects only native transactions involving the explicitly mapped company business into finance source intents. This integration does not run the TOML comparison suite or claim its benchmark scores. The coordinator persists intents and calls the native ERP source reconciler; it does not import Coffee books as company finance truth.
- **Economic ideas only (port/adapt, do not import the whole economy).** `environment.py` models each actor's cash, inventory, invoices, production/roasting lags, spoilage, bankruptcy, delivery loss/delay, consumer demand, and daily mechanics. `_seed_world()` in `main.py` hardcodes coffee roles, products, costs/caps/endowments. Its economics are coffee-specific and are not automotive supplier economics. Reuse as conceptual examples; port only a justified narrow rule after mapping its inputs/outputs to ERPNext and coordinator events.
- **Reset/replay.** Runs take configured seeds and construct a fresh environment in `main.py`; there is no general durable snapshot/reset API for a live `Environment`. `TimeManager.reset()` only zeroes the clock and does not reset marketplace, agents, RNG, books, or event queue. Repeatable trials therefore require a newly constructed seeded instance or coordinator-owned snapshot/reseed/replay implementation, not calling the clock reset.

### E-Commerce Bench: negotiation kernel, demand rules, and disruptions

- **Concrete reuse candidate — negotiation policy kernel.** `tools/opponent/simulator/counterpart.py` contains `CounterpartKernel`: parameterized deterministic counterpart responses, state/history and seeded Python/NumPy RNGs. `tools/opponent/kernel_manager.py` maps supplier/SKU to kernel family and reservation-price parameters, tracks per-pair negotiation state, and regenerates a kernel by order cycle. This is the best D1 candidate if kept behind a supplier-contract adapter. Do not expose hidden reservation prices/parameters to the tested agent. [Kernel](https://github.com/QwenLM/E-CommerceBench/blob/b84678071c424fc94713b0a9e1ae86c519bd74c7/tools/opponent/simulator/counterpart.py) · [manager](https://github.com/QwenLM/E-CommerceBench/blob/b84678071c424fc94713b0a9e1ae86c519bd74c7/tools/opponent/kernel_manager.py)
- **LLM dialogue is a separate rendering layer.** `supplier_llm.py` produces counterpart-facing text, while `KernelManager` chooses structured `Offer`/`Accept`/`Reject` decisions and price. Keep dialogue generation out of the authoritative offer/accept decision so that scripted scenario outcomes are reproducible and auditable.
- **Implemented selective reuse — native calendar supply signals.** The company background adapter constructs the pinned `EcommerceEnv` for each source date and uses native event/data methods to emit daily supplier demand and dated disruptions. Events update only the external supplier's private market through the company supplier path. The adapter emits no financial intents: the upstream runner's cash, stock, purchasing, delivery, fraud, returns, and settlement state are not projected as company records, and E-Commerce has no native AP schedule. ERPNext remains authoritative for the company's documents and finance.
- **Order and fraud behavior is entwined with a second economy.** `tools/opponent/order_processor.py` charges `bank_balance` immediately, creates its own pending delivery records, adjusts delivered quantities/quality, and later ties outcomes to the benchmark store/returns/settlement pipeline. That is not directly reusable for ERP procurement without creating conflicting cash, inventory and fulfillment truth. Extract only bounded behavior rules (e.g., a sampled short shipment or lead-time multiplier) and emit evidence/events for ERPNext/coordinator to reconcile.
- **Reset/reproducibility.** `EcommerceEnv(seed=...)` seeds a local RNG and the runner creates a fresh `EcommerceBenchAgent` for each run; `run.py` supports parallel run indices. The inspected source has no public `reset()` method for restoring an existing environment. A deterministic new instance is available, but exact replay also requires preserving seed, initial input/job, source commit, and any nondeterministic LLM outputs. The source includes seeded supplier kernels and per-run randomness; events are calendar CSV inputs. The coordinator still needs resettable scenario state and durable replay.

## C1 — supplier disagreement: contract implications

CoffeeBench can inform *how* independently scheduled businesses negotiate and message, but C1 needs explicit separate truth records. Model at least (1) buyer/ERP-side ordered and received quantities, (2) supplier-private dispatch/shipping evidence, and (3) shared communication/document references. A supplier response must derive from its private ledger, not read the buyer's ERP state or coordinator hidden scenario answer. Preserve both claims and their evidence; do not reconcile them by mutation or by importing CoffeeBench's deal/invoice callback. Route supplier email through the existing company Mailu environment (documented in `deploy/single-host/company-mail.md` and `deploy/single-host/mailu/`); CoffeeBench DMs are only a source pattern, not a new inbox stack. C1 acceptance should assert a supplier reply grounded in its own dispatch record, persisted evidence/correlation to the PO, and unchanged conflicting records until an authorized resolution.

The implemented Coffee supplier tool reads its private dispatch document and exposes the buyer-reported receipt separately; `stage_reply` accepts only facts retrieved in that turn. The supplier worker persists the Coffee transcript in its private SQLite database and returns a scoped conversation pointer, not the transcript or full private state. The coordinator applies only the typed kernel-state patch and retains ERP receipt truth.

## D1 — supplier price change: contract implications

E-CommerceBench's kernel computes counterpart negotiation behavior and a reservation/floor from supplier/SKU parameters, but the end-to-end order processor immediately debits its own bank balance and owns delivery, warehouse, returns and settlement. Reuse the structured negotiation decision kernel selectively, wrap it with an adapter that accepts canonical supplier identity, SKU, prior offers, scenario seed/cycle, and an externally visible quote request, then returns a quote/accept/reject plus traceable terms. Inject the supply disruption as a coordinator event that changes a future quote rule only; it must not rewrite an accepted ERP purchase order. Keep original PO terms immutable, persist revised quote as a separate version/offer, and require the existing authorization workflow before ERP contract/PO change. Do not import the benchmark's cash or stock mutation path. D1 should assert old PO price remains £50, the new offer reflects the changed scenario economics, and no ERP change occurs without authorization.

The implemented quote tool rebuilds the vendored `CounterpartKernel` from its seed and offer transcript, and uses current upstream E-Commerce event effects for availability, delivery delay, and future quote cost. Existing purchase-order unit price is visible contract context. Expedited shipping is a separate shipment-bound quote; the supplier confirms only a matching coordinator-provided grant after the native service commitment is present. The supplier worker does not post ERP actions or send email.

## Compatibility decision

Use both projects through **selective upstream integration**. CoffeeBench contributes its actual Agent harness and a complete pinned native company economy in the private background worker; E-CommerceBench contributes actual date-driven market signals through its native environment/data. Neither creates a second company financial ledger. `third_party/coffeebench/NOTICE.md` and `third_party/ecommercebench/NOTICE.md` retain pinned SHAs, licenses and hashes. Coffee's full 90-day background is not the original TOML experiment/evaluation runner. E-Commerce's market signals are not a run of its complete 365-day benchmark. The coordinator owns company event persistence, finance source reconciliation, ERPNext, approvals, Mailu, privacy and status.

## Audit method and boundaries

Cloned both public GitHub repositories at the SHAs above and inspected their actual tracked Python, project metadata, requirements, data files, and LICENSE files. Selected source is now vendored byte-for-byte under `third_party/` with hash and license notices. Component tests exercise actual E-Commerce calendar environment methods and a bounded Coffee native-environment advance with a fake model transport under the Coffee worker's Python 3.12 upstream lock. No live model-provider call, live email, full 90-day episode, full benchmark suite or native ERP write is included in that evidence. This report does not establish full benchmark score reproduction, native ERP end-to-end outcomes, or legal advice. Source links are commit-pinned so findings can be checked against the reviewed snapshots.
